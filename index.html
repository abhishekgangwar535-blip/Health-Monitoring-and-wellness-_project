Common viral illnesses

🌡VIRAL INFECTION

Flu (influenza)

Often starts suddenly with fever, aches, tiredness, headache, and cough. Higher-risk people should contact a clinician early.

Dengue

Fever with aches, headache, nausea, or rash may occur where dengue circulates. Warning signs can appear as fever settles.

Common cold

KNOW YOUR NUMBERS

Simple health checks

Ranges below are general adult references. Age, pregnancy, medicines, altitude, health conditions, and your usual baseline can change what is right for you.

CheckGeneral adult referenceWhen to get help
Blood pressureUnder 120/80 mmHg is normal; 130/80 or higher is in a high range.Repeated high readings: arrange a clinician review. Above 180 systolic and/or 120 diastolic: repeat after 1 minute. If still high, contact a clinician promptly; with chest pain, breathlessness, weakness, vision or speech changes, call emergency services.
TemperatureFever is commonly 38°C (100.4°F) or higher; method and age matter.Adult fever over 39.4°C (103°F) that persists/rises, or lasts over 48–72 hours: contact a clinician. Emergency symptoms include confusion, trouble breathing, stiff neck, seizure, or difficulty waking. Any fever in an infant under 3 months needs prompt medical advice.
Resting pulseUsually 60–100 beats per minute for a resting adult.Repeated unusual readings with fainting, chest pain, breathlessness, severe weakness, or confusion need urgent assessment. Athletes and some medicines can change baseline.
Oxygen (if measured)Many healthy people read 95–100% at sea level; personal baselines and altitude vary.Repeat a low reading with warm hands and stillness. Persistent 92% or lower, or any severe breathlessness/blue lips, needs emergency care. Follow your clinician's personal plan if you have chronic lung disease.
!
Know when it may be serious

Seek emergency help for severe trouble breathing, blue lips, chest pain, fainting, confusion, seizure, inability to wake, severe dehydration, heavy bleeding, or rapidly worsening symptoms. With possible dengue, urgent warning signs include severe belly pain, repeated vomiting, nose/gum bleeding, blood in vomit or stool, or extreme tiredness/restlessness—especially as fever goes away. Do not wait for a number to become severe if you feel seriously unwell.

i
Medicine safety comes first

There is no one-size-fits-all medicine for viral fever. Ask a clinician or pharmacist what is appropriate for age, symptoms, pregnancy, conditions, and other medicines. If dengue is possible, avoid aspirin and ibuprofen unless a clinician specifically directs you; these may increase bleeding risk. Never give aspirin to children or teenagers with a viral illness.
